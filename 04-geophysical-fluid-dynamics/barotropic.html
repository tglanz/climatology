15  The BVE on a rotating sphere

Teaching objects and equations prefixed P retain the companion physical-prerequisites numbering. Mathematical operators are introduced in vector calculus and integral theorems. Parcel derivatives and transport are developed in fluid kinematics.

How can one scalar field determine a non-divergent velocity everywhere on the sphere? We construct the surface operators and streamfunction, state the compatibility needed for inversion, and close the vorticity equation. Its local small-amplitude limit will then predict the direction and speed of Rossby-wave phase propagation.

15.1 Surface geometry and differential operators

Longitude and latitude are angles, so physical derivatives need radius and cosine factors. The spherical BVE cannot be obtained by relabeling Cartesian coordinates. (Arfken and Weber 2005, secs. 2.1–2.5; The Barotropic Vorticity Equation, n.d., sec. 1)

Definition P6.1 - Spherical coordinates, distance, and area

Let \(S_a^2\) denote the sphere of radius \(a\), with \(0\leq\lambda<2\pi\) and \(-\pi/2\leq\phi\leq\pi/2\). The tangent velocity is \(\mathbf{u}=u\hat{\mathbf{e}}_\lambda+v\hat{\mathbf{e}}_\phi\). At fixed latitude, arc length is \(a\cos\phi\,d\lambda\); meridional arc length is \(a\,d\phi\). Therefore

\[ ds^2=a^2\cos^2\phi\,d\lambda^2+a^2d\phi^2, \qquad dA=a^2\cos\phi\,d\phi\,d\lambda. \tag{P6.1} \]

Here \(ds\) is infinitesimal surface distance and \(dA\) is physical area.

Result P6.2 - Physical surface derivatives

For smooth scalar \(b\) and tangent velocity \(\mathbf{u}\) on the radius-\(a\) sphere, the gradient, divergence, normal curl, and surface Laplacian are

\[ \begin{aligned} \nabla_hb&=\hat{\mathbf{e}}_\lambda\frac{\partial_\lambda b}{a\cos\phi} +\hat{\mathbf{e}}_\phi\frac{\partial_\phi b}{a},\\ \nabla_h\cdot\mathbf{u}&=\frac{\partial_\lambda u+\partial_\phi(v\cos\phi)}{a\cos\phi},\\ \zeta&=\frac{\partial_\lambda v-\partial_\phi(u\cos\phi)}{a\cos\phi},\\ \nabla_h^2b&=\frac{\partial_\lambda^2b}{a^2\cos^2\phi} +\frac{1}{a^2\cos\phi}\partial_\phi(\cos\phi\,\partial_\phi b). \end{aligned}\tag{P6.2} \]

Derivation of Result P6.2

The divergence follows from fluxes through the sides of a small latitude-longitude cell divided by its area. The curl follows from circulation around the same cell. Inserting the gradient into divergence gives the last formula, the surface Laplacian or Laplace-Beltrami operator. Its units are inverse length squared. The apparent singularities at \(\cos\phi=0\) are coordinate singularities: physical fields must be smooth at the poles, rather than assigning arbitrary, longitude-dependent values there.

15.2 Streamfunction, vorticity inversion, and the Jacobian

A smooth non-divergent tangent field on the sphere is specified by one streamfunction, up to a constant. Recovering that streamfunction from vorticity closes the evolution. (Vallis 2006, sec. 4.2.1; The Barotropic Vorticity Equation, n.d., 1–2, 7–8)

Definition P6.3 - Streamfunction sign convention

Define the streamfunction \(\psi\) (\(\mathrm{m^2\,s^{-1}}\)) by

\[ \mathbf{u}=\hat{\mathbf{k}}\times\nabla_h\psi, \qquad u=-\frac{1}{a}\partial_\phi\psi, \qquad v=\frac{1}{a\cos\phi}\partial_\lambda\psi. \tag{P6.3} \]

Result P6.4 - Non-divergence and vorticity inversion

Conversely, every smooth non-divergent tangent velocity on the sphere admits a global single-valued streamfunction, up to an additive constant, as shown below. A smooth streamfunction defines a tangent velocity with \(\nabla_h\cdot\mathbf{u}=0\) and

\[ \zeta=\nabla_h^2\psi.\tag{P6.4} \]

Proof of Result P6.4

Substitution into (P6.2) cancels the mixed derivatives in divergence and gives (P6.4).

The velocity is tangent to contours of \(\psi\), since \(\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla_h\psi=0\). These contours are instantaneous streamlines; they are parcel trajectories only in steady flow. For completeness, prescribe \(d\psi=a\cos\phi\,v\,d\lambda-a u\,d\phi\). Non-divergence makes its integral around an infinitesimal cell zero, because \(\partial_\phi(a\cos\phi\,v)+\partial_\lambda(au)=0\). By the surface Stokes theorem the integral vanishes around every closed loop bounding a surface; every closed loop on the sphere can be contracted without meeting a hole. Thus path integrals from a fixed point define a global single-valued \(\psi\) up to a constant. Pole smoothness joins the coordinate charts without a physical singularity. Other domains need boundary and possible circulation information. In particular, constant mean velocity on a periodic plane is not recovered from periodic vorticity alone; here spherical topology avoids that undetermined harmonic flow. \(\square\)

Result P6.5 - Compatibility and gauge on a sphere

For a smooth globally defined non-divergent tangent velocity, the inversion \(\nabla_h^2\psi=\zeta\) requires zero spherical-mean relative vorticity. When this condition holds, a streamfunction is unique after imposing a zero-mean gauge:

\[ \int_{S_a^2}\zeta\,dA=0,\qquad \int_{S_a^2}\psi\,dA=0. \tag{P6.5} \]

Proof of Result P6.5

Equation (P6.4) is a Poisson equation. Integrating over the closed sphere gives the necessary compatibility condition \(\int_{S_a^2}\zeta\,dA=0\). A smooth globally defined tangent velocity satisfies this by Stokes’ theorem. The Laplacian annihilates constants, so \(\psi\) is unique only after fixing a gauge, as written in (P6.5). To see uniqueness, subtract two zero-mean solutions. Their difference \(d\) satisfies \(\nabla_h^2d=0\), so integration by parts gives \(\int|\nabla_hd|^2dA=0\). Thus \(d\) is constant, and its zero mean makes it zero. Existence can be constructed by the harmonic expansion in the companion chapter 8. \(\square\)

Definition P6.6 - Spherical Jacobian

For two scalars \(A,B\), define their spherical Jacobian

\[ J(A,B)=\frac{\partial_\lambda A\,\partial_\phi B -\partial_\phi A\,\partial_\lambda B}{a^2\cos\phi}. \tag{P6.6} \]

Inserting (P6.3) into \(\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla_hB\) gives exactly \(J(\psi,B)\). The sign convention is fixed by eastward \(u\) and northward \(v\); reversing the streamfunction convention would change multiple signs together.

15.3 Closed spherical equation and a sign check

The BVE is nonlinear vorticity advection coupled to an elliptic inversion. Planetary rotation contributes an exact linear longitude derivative on the sphere. (The Barotropic Vorticity Equation, n.d., eq. 11; Vallis 2006, sec. 4.4.2)

Result P6.7 - Closed spherical BVE

For the inviscid, unforced, constant-depth model on a rotating sphere, with \(f=2\Omega\sin\phi\) and the zero-mean inversion constraints,

\[ \partial_t\zeta+J(\psi,\zeta+f)=0, \qquad \nabla_h^2\psi=\zeta.\tag{P6.7} \]

Derivation of Result P6.7

Combining (P5.4) and (P6.4)-(P6.6), with \(f=2\Omega\sin\phi\), gives (P6.7).

Indeed \(\partial_\phi f=2\Omega\cos\phi\) and \(\partial_\lambda f=0\), so

\[ J(\psi,f)=\frac{2\Omega}{a^2}\partial_\lambda\psi, \qquad \partial_t\nabla_h^2\psi+J(\psi,\nabla_h^2\psi) +\frac{2\Omega}{a^2}\partial_\lambda\psi=0. \]

Remark P6.8 - Units and nonlinear closure

Every term in the vorticity equation has units \(\mathrm{s^{-2}}\). Although \(\psi\) appears, the nonlinearity is not a prescribed transport velocity: vorticity determines \(\psi\), and \(\psi\) determines the velocity advecting that same vorticity.

Example P6.9 - Solid rotation checks the spherical metric and sign

As a metric and sign check, choose a zonal solid rotation relative to the planet, \(u=U_s\cos\phi\), \(v=0\), with speed scale \(U_s\). Then

\[ \psi=-aU_s\sin\phi,\qquad \zeta=\frac{2U_s}{a}\sin\phi. \]

Both are smooth and have zero spherical mean. All fields are independent of longitude, so both Jacobians in (P6.7) vanish: this is an inviscid steady solution. Omitting the cosine term in the curl would fail this check.

15.4 Rossby waves from absolute-vorticity conservation

A meridional displacement changes planetary vorticity; compensating relative spin produces propagating Rossby waves. Their phase propagates westward relative to a resting background. (Vallis 2006, sec. 5.7)

Model P6.10 - Local beta-plane BVE

Use the local \(\beta\)-plane, where \(u=-\partial_y\psi\), \(v=\partial_x\psi\), \(\zeta=\nabla_h^2\psi\), and \(J(A,B)=A_xB_y-A_yB_x\). The BVE is

\[ \partial_t\nabla_h^2\psi+J(\psi,\nabla_h^2\psi)+\beta\partial_x\psi=0. \]

Assumption P6.11 - Uniform current and small disturbance

Let a constant mean eastward velocity be \(U\), and write \(\psi=-Uy+\psi'\), where \(\psi'\) is a small disturbance. The prime denotes a perturbation.

Definition P6.12 - Plane-wave amplitude, phase, and frequency

Seek a mode \(\psi'=\operatorname{Re}\{\Psi e^{i(k_xx+k_yy-\sigma t)}\}\), where \(\Psi\) is constant complex amplitude, \(k_x,k_y\) are wavenumbers, \(\sigma\) angular frequency, and \(\operatorname{Re}\) takes the real part. Put \(K^2=k_x^2+k_y^2>0\). The zonal phase speed is \(c_x=\sigma/k_x\) for \(k_x\ne0\).

Result P6.13 - Barotropic Rossby-wave dispersion

For the linearized, inviscid beta-plane model with uniform mean current \(U\) and \(K^2=k_x^2+k_y^2>0\),

\[ \sigma=Uk_x-\frac{\beta k_x}{k_x^2+k_y^2}, \qquad c_x=\frac{\sigma}{k_x}=U-\frac{\beta}{K^2}\quad(k_x\ne0). \tag{P6.8} \]

Derivation of Result P6.13

Since \(\nabla_h^2(-Uy)=0\), substitute \(\psi=-Uy+\psi'\) into the beta-plane BVE and discard products of disturbance amplitudes. This gives

\[ (\partial_t+U\partial_x)\nabla_h^2\psi'+\beta\partial_x\psi'=0. \]

For the plane wave, \(\partial_t\) acts as \(-i\sigma\), \(\partial_x\) as \(ik_x\), and \(\nabla_h^2\) as \(-K^2\). Inserting these into the linear disturbance equation gives \(iK^2(\sigma-Uk_x)+i\beta k_x=0\). Dividing by \(iK^2\) yields (P6.8).

Remark P6.14 - Phase propagation and limits

Here \(c_x\) is zonal phase speed. For \(\beta>0\), its intrinsic value \(c_x-U\) is negative. Phase speed describes constant-phase lines, not the velocity of a parcel or necessarily the propagation of a localized wave packet. Shorter waves have smaller intrinsic zonal phase speed in magnitude. At \(\beta=0\) and no background potential-vorticity gradient, the restoring term disappears. This result is a small-amplitude local-plane limit, not a replacement for the full spherical nonlinear equation.

15.5 Chapter summary

  • Sphere geometry sets the area element and all metric factors in (P6.1)-(P6.2). Pole smoothness replaces arbitrary polar grid values.
  • The streamfunction convention (P6.3) makes velocity non-divergent and gives \(\zeta=\nabla_h^2\psi\). Zero-mean compatibility and a gauge are necessary for inversion.
  • The spherical Jacobian is exactly non-divergent advection. The closed BVE is (P6.7).
  • Planetary advection is \(2\Omega\partial_\lambda\psi/a^2\). The local linear limit gives the Rossby-wave dispersion relation (P6.8).
