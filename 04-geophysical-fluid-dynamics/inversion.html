17  Spherical harmonics, inversion, and dissipation

Equations prefixed P retain the numbering of the companion physical-prerequisites document. Mathematical operators are introduced in vector calculus and integral theorems. Parcel derivatives and transport are developed in fluid kinematics.

17.1 Harmonics as Laplacian eigenfunctions

Result and motivation. Spherical harmonics diagonalize the surface Laplacian. They make streamfunction inversion and scale-selective damping algebraic operations. (Arfken and Weber 2005, secs. 9.3, 12.3–12.6; The Barotropic Vorticity Equation, n.d., sec. 3)

Define solid angle \(d\omega=dA/a^2=\cos\phi\,d\phi\,d\lambda\); the lower-case \(\omega\) in this measure is not the vorticity vector. A spherical harmonic \(Y_\ell^m\) is a smooth angular function satisfying

\[ \nabla_h^2Y_\ell^m=-\kappa_\ell^2Y_\ell^m, \qquad \kappa_\ell^2=\frac{\ell(\ell+1)}{a^2}, \quad \ell=0,1,\ldots,\quad -\ell\leq m\leq\ell. \tag{P8.1} \]

The nonnegative integer \(\ell\) is degree or total wavenumber; the integer \(m\) is order or zonal wavenumber. To obtain this structure, separate an angular eigenfunction as \(Y=\Theta(\phi)e^{im\lambda}\). Periodicity in longitude forces integer \(m\). Substitution into (P6.2), followed by \(\mu=\sin\phi\), gives

\[ \frac{d}{d\mu}\left[(1-\mu^2)\frac{d\Theta}{d\mu}\right] +\left[a^2\kappa^2-\frac{m^2}{1-\mu^2}\right]\Theta=0. \]

Regularity at both poles selects \(a^2\kappa^2=\ell(\ell+1)\) and associated Legendre functions \(P_\ell^m(\mu)\). This is an eigenvalue problem: allowed smooth functions occur only for particular eigenvalues. For \(m\geq0\), an explicit convention is

\[ \begin{aligned} P_\ell(\mu)&=\frac{1}{2^\ell\ell!}\frac{d^\ell}{d\mu^\ell}(\mu^2-1)^\ell,\\ P_\ell^m(\mu)&=(-1)^m(1-\mu^2)^{m/2}\frac{d^mP_\ell}{d\mu^m},\\ Y_\ell^m&=\left[\frac{2\ell+1}{4\pi}\frac{(\ell-m)!}{(\ell+m)!}\right]^{1/2} P_\ell^m(\sin\phi)e^{im\lambda}. \end{aligned} \]

Here \(n!\) denotes the product of integers from 1 to \(n\), with \(0!=1\). Negative orders are defined by \(Y_\ell^{-m}=(-1)^m\overline{Y_\ell^m}\); an overbar on a complex quantity means complex conjugation, whereas an overbar on a flow field in the companion chapter 7 meant zonal averaging. This is the standard orthonormal, Condon-Shortley phase convention:

\[ \begin{aligned} \int Y_\ell^m\overline{Y_{\ell'}^{m'}}\,d\omega&=\delta_{\ell\ell'}\delta_{mm'},\\ \zeta&=\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\sum_{m=-\ell}^{\ell}\widehat\zeta_\ell^mY_\ell^m, \qquad \widehat\zeta_\ell^m=\int\zeta\overline{Y_\ell^m}\,d\omega. \end{aligned}\tag{P8.2} \]

The Kronecker deltas are 1 when their indices agree and 0 otherwise. A real field requires \(\widehat\zeta_\ell^{-m}=(-1)^m\overline{\widehat\zeta_\ell^m}\). The supplied barotropic notes use a different normalization and phase pairing (their eqs. 15-17). Their coefficient values and conjugacy rule must be interpreted in their own convention; the physical field and eigenvalues are unchanged. In particular, the notes’ constant harmonic is \(1/\sqrt2\), whereas (P8.2) uses \(1/\sqrt{4\pi}\).

17.2 Inversion and the spectral energy weights

Result and motivation. Nonconstant vorticity modes determine streamfunction modes uniquely. The constant mode cannot be inverted and must be absent from relative vorticity. (The Barotropic Vorticity Equation, n.d., 1, 7–8; Arfken and Weber 2005, secs. 10.4–10.5)

Insert the expansion (P8.2) into \(\nabla_h^2\psi=\zeta\) and match orthogonal coefficients:

\[ \widehat\psi_\ell^m=-\frac{a^2}{\ell(\ell+1)}\widehat\zeta_\ell^m \quad(\ell\geq1),\qquad \widehat\zeta_0^0=0,\qquad\widehat\psi_0^0=0. \tag{P8.3} \]

The last equality fixes the gauge, while the preceding zero is a compatibility condition. They are logically different. A constant added to \(\psi\) changes no velocity. A constant added to relative vorticity cannot be the curl of a smooth globally defined tangent flow on the sphere.

Orthogonality gives Parseval’s identity, \(\int|\zeta|^2d\omega=\sum_{\ell,m}|\widehat\zeta_\ell^m|^2\). Since \(dA=a^2d\omega\), (P7.2) becomes

\[ E=\frac{a^4}{2}\sum_{\ell\geq1,m} \frac{|\widehat\zeta_\ell^m|^2}{\ell(\ell+1)}, \qquad Z=\frac{a^2}{2}\sum_{\ell\geq1,m}|\widehat\zeta_\ell^m|^2. \tag{P8.4} \]

Define \(E_\ell,Z_\ell\) by the corresponding sums over \(m\) at fixed \(\ell\). Then \(Z_\ell=\kappa_\ell^2E_\ell\), as used in (P7.3). Larger \(\ell\) means smaller horizontal structure; \(a/\sqrt{\ell(\ell+1)}\) is an inverse-wavenumber length, not a unique wavelength or grid spacing.

17.3 Truncation and evaluation of nonlinear transport

Result and motivation. A finite harmonic expansion defines the represented state, while a sufficiently fine transform grid evaluates the nonlinear term. These are different resolutions. (The Barotropic Vorticity Equation, n.d., 4–8)

Triangular truncation at degree \(N\) retains \(0\leq\ell\leq N\) and all \(|m|\leq\ell\), often denoted T\(N\). There are \((N+1)^2\) real degrees of freedom for a real scalar before imposing its mean constraint. Define \(\mathcal{P}_N\) to be orthogonal projection onto these retained modes. The ideal projected equation is

\[ \partial_t\zeta_N=-\mathcal{P}_NJ(\psi_N,\zeta_N+f), \qquad \nabla_h^2\psi_N=\zeta_N. \]

The subscript \(N\) denotes a truncated field. The nonlinear product produces degrees outside the retained set. One must compute its projection, rather than assume that the product is itself truncated.

A pseudospectral or spectral-transform method inverts vorticity in harmonic space, transforms to a longitude-latitude grid, forms velocity-vorticity products there, and transforms the resulting tendency back. Sampling too coarsely causes aliasing: unresolved product modes masquerade as retained modes. For the triangular spherical transform model in the notes, the quadratic de-aliasing requirements are

\[ N_\lambda\geq3N+1,\qquad N_\phi\geq\left\lceil\frac{3N+1}{2}\right\rceil. \tag{P8.5} \]

Here \(N_\lambda\) is the number of equally spaced longitudes, \(N_\phi\) the number of Gaussian latitudes, and \(\lceil x\rceil\) the smallest integer at least \(x\). Gaussian latitudes are \(\phi_j=\arcsin\mu_j\), where \(\mu_j\) are roots of the Legendre polynomial \(P_{N_\phi}\). Their weights \(w_j>0\) give \(\int_{-1}^{1}g(\mu)d\mu=\sum_jw_jg(\mu_j)\) for polynomial \(g\) of degree at most \(2N_\phi-1\). Since \(d\omega=d\lambda\,d\mu\), this quadrature is adapted to the spherical transform.

In longitude, two factors with wavenumbers at most \(N\) have product modes up to \(2N\). Projecting onto a mode up to \(N\) involves integration of a trigonometric polynomial with wavenumbers up to \(3N\); more than \(3N\) equally spaced points avoid spurious nonzero discrete averages. The latitude requirement in (P8.5) is the spherical-transform counterpart derived in the supplied notes. T85 therefore admits \(N_\lambda=256\), \(N_\phi=128\). These conditions concern quadratic products; they do not guarantee that arbitrary localized functions, higher nonlinearities, or time integration are exact.

17.4 Drag, hyperdiffusion, and their signs

Result and motivation. Dissipation should damp every retained nonconstant mode. Writing it with the nonnegative operator \(-\nabla_h^2\) makes this sign transparent. (The Barotropic Vorticity Equation, n.d., eq. 12 and pp. 3-4, 11)

Let \(r\geq0\) be a linear drag rate, \(p\) a positive integer diffusion order, and \(\nu_p\geq0\) a hyperdiffusion coefficient (\(\mathrm{m^{2p}\,s^{-1}}\)). Define the vorticity dissipation operator

\[ \mathcal{D}(\zeta)=-r\zeta-\nu_p(-\nabla_h^2)^p\zeta. \tag{P8.6} \]

For \(p=1\), the second term is ordinary diffusion \(+\nu_1\nabla_h^2\zeta\). For \(p=2\), it is \(-\nu_2\nabla_h^4\zeta\), called biharmonic diffusion. Its origin here is a small-scale model closure; it is not an exact substitute for unresolved three-dimensional turbulence. On each mode,

\[ \partial_t\widehat\zeta_\ell^m\big|_{\mathcal D} =-\gamma_\ell\widehat\zeta_\ell^m, \qquad \gamma_\ell=r+\nu_p\kappa_\ell^{2p}\geq0. \tag{P8.7} \]

A pure damped mode decays as \(e^{-\gamma_\ell t}\), and its squared amplitude decays as \(e^{-2\gamma_\ell t}\). Both energy and relative enstrophy decrease under this operator. Increasing \(p\) concentrates damping near the high-degree end. An alternative specifies a cutoff damping rate \(\gamma_*>0\) and sets \(\nu_p=\gamma_*/\kappa_N^{2p}\), so the hyperdiffusive damping rate is \(\gamma_*[\ell(\ell+1)/(N(N+1))]^p\). A cutoff damping rate has units \(\mathrm{s^{-1}}\) and must not be confused with the dimensional \(\nu_p\).

17.5 Chapter summary

  • Harmonics \(Y_\ell^m\) are smooth Laplacian eigenfunctions with eigenvalues \(-\ell(\ell+1)/a^2\). Normalization and phase conventions affect coefficients.
  • Inversion is division by those eigenvalues for \(\ell\geq1\). The zero vorticity mode is excluded; the zero streamfunction mode is a gauge.
  • Spectral energy gives more weight to large spatial scales than enstrophy. Their exact weights are (P8.4).
  • T\(N\) truncation and the transform grid are distinct. Adequate Gaussian and longitude sampling prevent quadratic aliasing under the stated transform assumptions.
  • Linear drag and hyperdiffusion give nonnegative modal damping rates (P8.7). Their coefficients have different units and scale dependence.
