14  A shallow layer and the barotropic reduction

Teaching objects and equations prefixed P retain the companion physical-prerequisites numbering. Mathematical operators are introduced in vector calculus and integral theorems. Parcel derivatives and transport are developed in fluid kinematics.

Why is absolute vorticity conserved in the BVE but not in every incompressible layer? A column can conserve volume while changing horizontal area and thickness. We first derive the thickness-normalized invariant, then identify the extra restrictions that turn it into the constant-depth barotropic equation.

14.1 Layer momentum and thickness conservation

A homogeneous shallow layer can still stretch vertically. Its conserved scalar includes layer thickness, which must become constant before absolute vorticity itself is materially conserved. (Vallis 2006, secs. 3.1, 3.6)

Model P5.1 - Homogeneous shallow-water layer

Consider a constant-density layer on a local rotating plane. A stationary flat bottom is at \(z=0\) and a free surface at \(z=h(x,y,t)>0\). The layer thickness is \(h\) (\(\mathrm{m}\)). Assume hydrostatic pressure, small depth-to-horizontal-scale ratio, and horizontal velocity \(\mathbf{u}(x,y,t)=(u,v)\) independent of depth to leading order. Pressure at the surface is a spatial constant \(p_s\), so integration of (P3.6) gives \(p=p_s+\rho_0g(h-z)\) and \(\nabla_hp/\rho_0=g\nabla_hh\) at fixed \(z\).

Define the horizontal material derivative \(D_h/Dt=\partial_t+u\partial_x+v\partial_y\). In the absence of forcing and friction, the shallow-water equations are

\[ \begin{aligned} \frac{D_hu}{Dt}-fv&=-g\partial_xh,\\ \frac{D_hv}{Dt}+fu&=-g\partial_yh,\\ \partial_th+\nabla_h\cdot(h\mathbf{u})&=0, \quad\text{or}\quad \frac{D_hh}{Dt}=-h\nabla_h\cdot\mathbf{u}. \end{aligned}\tag{P5.1} \]

Remark P5.2 - Column conservation permits horizontal convergence

The third equation follows by conserving the volume \(h\delta A\) of each moving column. The surface is material, so its upward motion is compatible with horizontal convergence. Even though the three-dimensional fluid is incompressible, the depth-independent horizontal velocity may have nonzero divergence because thickness changes.

14.2 Potential vorticity and vortex stretching

The shallow-water potential vorticity is normal absolute vorticity divided by thickness. Stretching a column amplifies its absolute vorticity. (Vallis 2006, sec. 3.6.1, eqs. 3.77-3.78)

Definition P5.3 - Layer divergence and relative vorticity

Define horizontal divergence \(\delta=\partial_xu+\partial_yv\) and vertical relative vorticity \(\zeta=\partial_xv-\partial_yu\).

Result P5.4 - Layer stretching of absolute vorticity

For the inviscid homogeneous shallow-water equations (P5.1) with \(f=f(y)\) independent of time,

\[ \frac{D_h(\zeta+f)}{Dt}=-(\zeta+f)\delta. \tag{P5.2} \]

Derivation of Result P5.4

Take \(\partial_x\) of the northward momentum equation minus \(\partial_y\) of the eastward equation. Mixed pressure derivatives cancel. Differentiating the advective terms gives

\[ \partial_x(D_hv/Dt)-\partial_y(D_hu/Dt) =\frac{D_h\zeta}{Dt}+\zeta\delta. \]

For \(f=f(y)\) the Coriolis derivatives give \(f\delta+v\partial_yf\). Since \(\partial_tf=0\), collecting these terms gives (P5.2).

Definition P5.5 - Shallow-water potential vorticity

Define \(q=(\zeta+f)/h\), with units \(\mathrm{m^{-1}\,s^{-1}}\), for positive layer thickness \(h\).

Result P5.6 - Conservation of layer potential vorticity

Under the preceding inviscid shallow-water assumptions, \(q\) is conserved along each horizontal column trajectory:

\[ \frac{D_hq}{Dt} =\frac{1}{h}\frac{D_h(\zeta+f)}{Dt} -\frac{\zeta+f}{h^2}\frac{D_hh}{Dt}=0. \tag{P5.3} \]

Derivation of Result P5.6

Use the quotient rule displayed in (P5.3), then substitute \(D_h(\zeta+f)/Dt=-(\zeta+f)\delta\) and \(D_hh/Dt=-h\delta\). The two terms cancel exactly.

Remark P5.7 - Stretching, circulation, and bottom topography

A converging column has \(\delta<0\), grows thicker, and increases its absolute vorticity in the same ratio, leaving \(q\) unchanged. Equivalently, Kelvin’s theorem conserves \((\zeta+f)\delta A\), while volume conservation fixes \(h\delta A\); dividing the two gives (P5.3). With a stationary uneven bottom, thickness is surface elevation minus bottom elevation, so topography can affect \(q\) even beneath a rigid upper boundary. That case is excluded from the constant-depth model below. This layer \(q\) is not the general three-dimensional potential vorticity of a stratified compressible atmosphere.

Example P5.8 - A thickening column on an f-plane

Initially let \(h=H>0\) and \(\zeta=0\) at constant \(f=f_0\). If horizontal convergence increases thickness to \(5H/4\) without forcing or friction, potential-vorticity conservation gives \((\zeta+f_0)/(5H/4)=f_0/H\). Hence \(\zeta=f_0/4\). A Northern Hemisphere column becomes cyclonic (its relative vorticity has the same sign as \(f_0\)) despite three-dimensional incompressibility: its absolute vorticity, rather than its density, increases during the stretching.

14.3 Constant depth and the non-divergent BVE

With constant layer thickness, potential-vorticity conservation reduces to advection of absolute vorticity. Pressure remains a constraint force under a rigid lid. (Vallis 2006, secs. 3.1.3, 4.4.2; The Barotropic Vorticity Equation, n.d., 1–2)

Model P5.9 - Rigid-lid layer of fixed depth

Impose a rigid lid, flat bottom, and a fixed thickness \(h=H\) everywhere, with \(H>0\) constant. Then (P5.1) gives \(\nabla_h\cdot\mathbf{u}=0\). The pressure under a rigid lid adjusts to maintain that constraint; it is not the constant surface pressure of the free-surface example. It would be inconsistent to put \(h=H\) directly into every free-surface pressure term in (P5.1) and conclude that all horizontal pressure gradients vanish.

Definition P5.10 - Normal absolute vorticity

Let \(Q=\zeta+f\) be normal absolute vorticity (\(\mathrm{s^{-1}}\)). It differs by the constant factor \(H\) from the layer potential vorticity \(q\). It will be the parcel invariant of the constant-depth model.

Result P5.11 - Non-divergent barotropic vorticity equation

For the shallow layer of Model P5.1, with depth-independent two-dimensional tangent velocity, homogeneous density, fixed planetary rotation, fixed thickness, a flat bottom, rigid lid, and no viscosity or non-conservative force,

\[ \partial_t\zeta+\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla_h(\zeta+f)=0, \qquad \nabla_h\cdot\mathbf{u}=0. \tag{P5.4} \]

Derivation of Result P5.11

It follows equally from (P5.2) with \(\delta=0\), or from circulation conservation on an area-preserving surface. There is no need to assume geostrophic balance in (P5.4).

Remark P5.12 - Scope of the barotropic reduction

The restrictions used are a homogeneous fluid, two-dimensional tangent velocity, no thickness variation or topographic stretching, constant planetary rotation, no non-conservative forces, and horizontal non-divergence. They remove density-pressure production, vertical shear and tilting dynamics of a stratified layer, and vortex stretching associated with thickness changes. The resulting model supports horizontal eddy dynamics and Rossby waves. It does not prognose temperature, stratification, acoustic waves, or free-surface gravity waves.

Remark P5.13 - Steady planetary rotation is not a zero parcel derivative

Because \(f\) varies in space, \(D_hQ/Dt=0\) means

\[ \frac{D_h\zeta}{Dt}=-\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla_h f. \]

Time independence gives \(\partial_tf=0\), not \(D_hf/Dt=0\). This distinction corrects a misleading sentence on p. 2 of the supplied notes; their subsequent BVE retains the correct planetary-advection term.

14.4 Chapter summary

  • Layer thickness \(h\) satisfies volume conservation (P5.1). Horizontal convergence can coexist with three-dimensional incompressibility.
  • Absolute vorticity obeys the stretching law (P5.2). Layer potential vorticity \(q=(\zeta+f)/h\) is materially conserved.
  • A rigid lid, flat bottom, and fixed thickness enforce horizontal non-divergence. The pressure adjusts to impose that constraint.
  • The non-divergent BVE (P5.4) conserves \(Q=\zeta+f\) on parcels. It is a two-dimensional dynamical model, not a complete thermodynamic atmosphere.

14.5.1 Layer potential vorticity

Exercise P5.1 - Thickness and latitude change together (intermediate)

A parcel column initially has \((h_1,\zeta_1,f_1)\) and later \((h_2,\zeta_2,f_2)\). Assuming layer potential vorticity is conserved and both thicknesses are positive, solve for \(\zeta_2\).

14.5.2 Constant-depth reduction

Exercise P5.2 - A rigid lid above topography (model evaluation)

A fixed upper boundary lies above a stationary uneven bottom. Does a rigid lid alone imply constant thickness or horizontal non-divergence?

Exercise P5.3 - Relative vorticity on a beta-plane (straightforward)

For the unforced non-divergent BVE with northward velocity \(v\), find \(D_h\zeta/Dt\) and explain why \(\partial_tf=0\) is insufficient to set it to zero.
