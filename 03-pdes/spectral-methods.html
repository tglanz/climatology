10  Spectral numerical methods

A spectral method approximates a field by a finite sum of global basis functions. It solves for their coefficients rather than for independent values in each cell. The basis should respect the domain: Fourier modes suit periodic intervals, orthogonal polynomials suit bounded intervals, and spherical harmonics suit the sphere. The function-space chapter explains projection; here we turn projection into a numerical method.

10.1 From a PDE to coefficient equations

Let \(u(x,t)\) be a scalar field on a domain \(D\), and let \(\{\varphi_j\}\) be an orthonormal basis for a specified inner product \(\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle\). For \(J\) retained functions, write

\[ u_J(x,t)=\sum_{j=0}^{J-1}a_j(t)\varphi_j(x). \]

For a PDE \(\partial_t u=\mathcal{L}u+\mathcal{N}(u)+g\), a Galerkin method requires the residual to be orthogonal to every retained basis function:

\[ \frac{da_i}{dt} =\sum_{j=0}^{J-1}\langle\mathcal{L}\varphi_j,\varphi_i\rangle a_j +\langle\mathcal{N}(u_J),\varphi_i\rangle +\langle g,\varphi_i\rangle. \]

Here \(\mathcal{L}\) is linear, \(\mathcal{N}\) contains nonlinear terms, and \(g\) is forcing. A nonorthonormal basis introduces a mass matrix \(M_{ij}=\langle\varphi_j,\varphi_i\rangle\) on the left. Boundary conditions must be built into the basis or imposed separately. A collocation method instead sets the residual to zero at selected nodes. A pseudospectral method evaluates terms through transformations between coefficients and node values. With adequate quadrature and dealiasing, its computed nonlinear projection can agree with Galerkin projection. These distinctions concern spatial discretization; time integration is a separate numerical choice. See Boyd (2001) for the general framework.

For a periodic interval \([0,2\pi)\), \(\partial_x e^{ikx}=ik e^{ikx}\) and \(\partial_x^2e^{ikx}=-k^2e^{ikx}\). Differentiation becomes coefficient multiplication. With physical coordinate \(s=Rx\), the derivative is instead \(\partial_s=R^{-1}\partial_x\), so its units are inverse length.

10.2 Why global expansions can be accurate

For an orthonormal basis, the squared \(L^2\) truncation error is the energy in omitted coefficients. Rapid coefficient decay therefore gives rapid convergence. For periodic analytic functions that extend analytically into a complex strip, Fourier coefficients decay exponentially with mode number. Finite regularity usually gives algebraic decay. Discontinuities cause oscillations near jumps and slow convergence; increasing the truncation alone does not remove the Gibbs phenomenon. Spectral accuracy depends on regularity and compatible boundary conditions, not merely on choosing a transform.

A global method is useful when smooth solutions need high accuracy, derivatives or elliptic inversions have simple coefficient formulas, or scale-dependent analysis is central. Local methods may be preferable for shocks, complex boundaries, local refinement, or localized communication.

10.3 Spherical harmonic representation

Use colatitude \(\theta\in[0,\pi]\), longitude \(\phi\in[0,2\pi)\), latitude \(\alpha=\pi/2-\theta\), and radius \(R>0\) in metres. Define \(\mu=\cos\theta=\sin\alpha\). Solid angle is dimensionless:

\[ d\Omega=\sin\theta\,d\theta\,d\phi=d\mu\,d\phi \]

when the \(\mu\) integral runs from \(-1\) to \(1\). Physical area is \(R^2d\Omega\). Use the orthonormal complex harmonics from the spherical harmonics chapter. For \(m\ge0\), their associated Legendre functions include the Condon–Shortley phase:

\[ P_\ell^m(\mu)=(-1)^m(1-\mu^2)^{m/2} \frac{d^mP_\ell}{d\mu^m}, \qquad Y_\ell^m=N_{\ell m}P_\ell^m(\mu)e^{im\phi}, \quad N_{\ell m}=\sqrt{\frac{2\ell+1}{4\pi}\frac{(\ell-m)!}{(\ell+m)!}}. \]

Negative orders satisfy \(Y_\ell^{-m}=(-1)^m\overline{Y_\ell^m}\). Triangular truncation at degree \(L\) retains

\[ f_L=\sum_{\ell=0}^{L}\sum_{m=-\ell}^{\ell} \widehat f_\ell^mY_\ell^m, \qquad \widehat f_\ell^m=\int_{S^2}f\overline{Y_\ell^m}\,d\Omega. \]

There are \((L+1)^2\) complex basis functions. A real field obeys \(\widehat f_\ell^{-m}=(-1)^m\overline{\widehat f_\ell^m}\), reducing independent storage. If \(f\) has units \(U\), so do its coefficients under this dimensionless normalization. Truncation degree and the number of stored modes differ by one when degree zero is included.

10.4 Gaussian grid and quadrature

A quadrature rule approximates an integral by weighted function values. The \(n\)-point Gauss–Legendre rule on \([-1,1]\) uses the roots \(\mu_i\) of the Legendre polynomial \(P_n\) and positive weights

\[ \int_{-1}^{1}q(\mu)\,d\mu \approx\sum_{i=1}^{n}w_iq(\mu_i), \qquad w_i=\frac{2}{(1-\mu_i^2)[P_n'(\mu_i)]^2}. \]

It is exact for polynomials of degree at most \(2n-1\), and \(\sum_iw_i=2\). Golub and Welsch (1969) gives algorithms for computing Gaussian rules from orthogonal-polynomial recurrences. The following argument explains why the Legendre nodes give this exactness.

For a polynomial \(q\) of degree at most \(2n-1\), polynomial division gives \(q=P_ns+r\), where \(s\) and \(r\) have degree at most \(n-1\). Legendre orthogonality makes \(\int P_ns=0\). At every node \(P_n(\mu_i)=0\), so \(q(\mu_i)=r(\mu_i)\). Choose weights that integrate the Lagrange interpolant of \(r\) exactly. The same weighted sum then integrates \(q\) exactly. The extra exact degrees come from orthogonality, not from uniformly spacing the nodes.

A full Gaussian grid combines these nodes with \(M\) equally spaced longitudes:

\[ \alpha_i=\arcsin\mu_i,\qquad \phi_j=\frac{2\pi j}{M},\quad j=0,\ldots,M-1. \]

Latitudes are not equally spaced, and no node is at a pole. An equatorial node exists when \(n\) is odd. Every latitude ring has the same \(M\) longitudes; a reduced Gaussian grid instead varies their count and needs a corresponding transform algorithm. The full-grid formulas below do not apply unchanged to a reduced grid.

The area integral and global mean are approximated by

\[ \int_{S_R^2}f\,dA \approx R^2\frac{2\pi}{M}\sum_{i,j}w_if_{ij}, \qquad \langle f\rangle_A\approx\frac{1}{2M}\sum_{i,j}w_if_{ij}. \]

Thus a constant integrates to \(4\pi R^2\). For a zonal profile, normalized latitude weights are \(w_i/2\). Do not multiply Gaussian weights by \(\cos\alpha_i\) again: the substitution \(d\mu=\cos\alpha\,d\alpha\) already accounts for the area Jacobian. Uniform weights per latitude ring would also be incorrect.

10.4.1 Worked quadrature example

For \(n=2\), the nodes are \(\mu=\pm1/\sqrt3\) and both weights are one. For \(f=\mu^2\) the approximate global mean is

\[ \frac12\left[\left(-\frac1{\sqrt3}\right)^2+ \left(\frac1{\sqrt3}\right)^2\right]=\frac13, \]

which equals \(\frac12\int_{-1}^{1}\mu^2\,d\mu\). The same rule gives \(1/9\) for the mean of \(\mu^4\), whereas its exact mean is \(1/5\). Degree four exceeds this rule’s degree-three guarantee.

10.5 Why this grid helps the SHT

The spherical harmonic transform (SHT) separates into longitude Fourier analysis and latitude Legendre analysis. For \(m\ge0\), define

\[ F_m(\mu_i)=\frac1M\sum_{j=0}^{M-1}f(\mu_i,\phi_j)e^{-im\phi_j}. \]

Then compute

\[ \widehat f_\ell^m\approx 2\pi N_{\ell m}\sum_{i=1}^{n}w_iF_m(\mu_i)P_\ell^m(\mu_i). \]

An FFT computes all longitude coefficients on each ring. Associated Legendre sums complete the forward transform; synthesis evaluates the harmonic sum at nodes. See Schaeffer (2013) for a practical transform algorithm.

For a field bandlimited to degree \(L\), sufficient sampling conditions for exact coefficient recovery in exact arithmetic are

\[ n\ge L+1,\qquad M\ge 2L+1. \]

To see why, expand the input in retained harmonics. The longitude sum contains \(e^{i(m'-m)\phi_j}\) and is zero unless \(m'=m\) under this sampling condition. For the surviving terms,

\[ P_\ell^m(\mu)P_{\ell'}^m(\mu) =(1-\mu^2)^m\frac{d^mP_\ell}{d\mu^m} \frac{d^mP_{\ell'}}{d\mu^m}, \]

which is a polynomial of degree at most \(\ell+\ell'\le2L\). Since \(2n-1\ge2L\), Gaussian quadrature integrates these products exactly. The discrete inner product therefore reproduces orthogonality on the retained harmonic space. This is why the nodes and weights are part of the transform definition.

The statement does not claim that each \(P_\ell^m\) is itself a polynomial: odd \(m\) introduces a square root. The same-order product is polynomial. Nor does it promise exact coefficients for arbitrary sampled fields with energy above \(L\). Such unresolved structure can alias into retained modes.

For the bandlimited field, discrete Parseval also follows:

\[ \frac{2\pi}{M}\sum_{i,j}w_i|f_L(\mu_i,\phi_j)|^2 =\sum_{\ell=0}^{L}\sum_{m=-\ell}^{\ell}|\widehat f_\ell^m|^2. \]

Round trips and this identity test normalization, weights, and coordinate order. Reversing latitude requires reversing the data and weights together. A shifted longitude origin requires consistent Fourier phases. Shape agreement alone does not establish transform compatibility.

A direct separated implementation uses approximately \(O(nM\log M)\) work for longitude FFTs and \(O(nL^2)\) for the Legendre stage. For \(n,M=O(L)\) the latter is cubic. An SHT is not simply a two-dimensional FFT. Recurrences, symmetry, precomputation, and specialized algorithms change practical costs and memory needs.

10.6 Solving equations in spectral space

On a radius-\(R\) sphere, the physical Laplace–Beltrami operator satisfies

\[ \Delta_{S_R^2}Y_\ell^m=-\frac{\ell(\ell+1)}{R^2}Y_\ell^m. \]

For Poisson’s equation \(\Delta_{S_R^2}u=f\), the sphere has no boundary and a constant is in the nullspace. A solution exists only when \(\int f\,d\Omega=0\). Fix the undetermined mean by setting \(\widehat u_0^0=0\). For every \(\ell\ge1\),

\[ \widehat u_\ell^m=-\frac{R^2}{\ell(\ell+1)}\widehat f_\ell^m. \]

For example, if \(f=A Y_2^0\), then \(u=-AR^2Y_2^0/6\). Applying the Laplacian recovers \(f\), checking the sign and radius factor. If \(f\) has units \(U\,\mathrm{m}^{-2}\), \(u\) has units \(U\).

For diffusion \(\partial_t u=\kappa\Delta_{S_R^2}u\), with diffusivity \(\kappa\) in \(\mathrm{m}^2\mathrm{s}^{-1}\),

\[ \widehat u_\ell^m(t)=\widehat u_\ell^m(0) \exp\left[-\frac{\kappa\ell(\ell+1)}{R^2}t\right]. \]

The mean remains constant and smaller scales decay faster. If forward Euler is used instead of this exact update, stability for all retained diffusion modes requires \(\Delta t\le2R^2/[\kappa L(L+1)]\) for \(L\ge1\). Accurate spatial differentiation does not remove time-step restrictions.

10.7 Nonlinear terms and aliasing

A product of fields bandlimited to \(L\) can contain degrees up to \(2L\). Computing it on the minimally sampled grid and transforming back can fold unresolved modes into lower degrees. In a periodic example, grid samples cannot distinguish Fourier modes \(k\) and \(k+M\) because \(e^{i(k+M)2\pi j/M}=e^{ik2\pi j/M}\).

For exact projection of a product of two scalar degree-\(L\) fields onto degrees through \(L\), a sufficient full Gaussian grid satisfies

\[ M>3L,\qquad 2n-1\ge3L. \]

The longitude integrand contains three mode factors. Its maximum frequency is \(3L\). Surviving latitude products also have degree at most \(3L\): the longitude selection rule makes the sum of their absolute orders even, so the factors of \((1-\mu^2)^{1/2}\) combine into an integer power. Gaussian exactness then applies. This is the spherical counterpart of padding each dimension by roughly \(3/2\) for a quadratic product. Strict inequalities and integer rounding matter. Derivatives of vector components and higher-order nonlinearities require their own analysis; these bounds apply to scalar products as stated.

A pseudospectral step therefore synthesizes on an adequate grid, evaluates the nonlinear expression there, analyzes it, and retains only the intended modes. Filtering damps selected coefficients but does not retroactively make an aliased projection exact. Dealiasing, physical diffusion, and numerical filtering serve different purposes.

10.8 Uses and numerical checks

Spectral coefficients support elliptic inversion, diffusion, scale-dependent filtering, energy spectra, and comparison across resolutions. A scalar isotropic linear filter multiplies all orders at degree \(\ell\) by the same factor; this preserves rotational symmetry. General position-dependent coefficients couple modes and need not yield diagonal operators.

Before using a transform in a solver, verify a constant, an individual harmonic, a forward/inverse round trip, and discrete Parseval. Check an analytic PDE solution such as the diffusion mode above. Increase truncation and quadrature resolution separately: convergence in one does not demonstrate convergence in the other. Record basis normalization, phase convention, radius, retained degrees, node coordinates, and weights so that stored coefficients remain interpretable.
